Three ways to show the projects

P6-01 for Michael, 2026-10-21. A recommendation, not a build: nothing on the site changes with this file.

The projects page today is a grid of cards with category chips. This compares three ways to rebuild it: one graph for projects and stack, a grid of case files, and a dated log. Each option has the same page drawn twice in the site's own colours (a 1280 px desktop screen and a 390 px phone screen), then what a hiring manager takes from it in 10 seconds, what it costs on the data the site already has, who has done it well, and what could go wrong. The recommendation is at the end.

What the page has to work with, from src/lib/data/projects.ts: 10 projects, 8 of them featured; 4 with a full case study; 9 with an authored architecture diagram; no project thumbnails yet, and the brand rules forbid stock or fake screenshots (docs/notes/brand.md). The readers, in order: hiring managers and recruiters, then MAXX staff, then people Michael meets at conferences, usually on a phone (brand.md, Positioning).

A

One graph for projects and stack

The projects become nodes beside the stack graph from /skills. Picking one lights the parts it was built from and opens a panel that stays on the graph.

What a hiring manager sees in 10 s

Before any click: six cluster names, a column of project titles and a lot of dots. The pitch is that the stack and the work are one picture, which suits "Agentic Systems Engineer". But nothing in the first 10 seconds says what any project achieved: the numbers wait behind a click. A recruiter who doesn't click leaves with a list of titles.

Cost to build on the current data

Reuses: every project:<slug> edge in skills.ts (already written for the /skills project chips), layoutGraph, edgePaths and projectChain in lib/graph/layout.ts, all of lib/graph/view.ts, nodesOf in lib/graph/edges.ts, and SkillGraph's selection state. From projects.ts: slug, title, company, shortDescription, metrics, githubUrl, featured, order, caseStudy.timeframe.

New: a Projects container in layoutGraph, which breaks its 3 × 2 and 2 × 3 cluster grids, so the layout is redrawn at both column counts; if projects have to sit near the parts they use, the hand layout stops working and D99 says to reconsider d3-force (a new runtime dependency, pre-approved at an exact pin); a ProjectPanel (side panel at desktop, bottom sheet on phones) with focus handling that doesn't fight the concierge panel; a ?project=<slug> URL so a selection can be shared; the List view grows a projects section; tests for each.

Size: large. It is P5 again with one more container and a panel, and P5 took seven harness features (feature_list.json, P5-01 to P5-07).

What others have done

  • Quartz, Graph View: a static-site generator that draws, on each page, a local graph of the notes linking to and from it, with the global graph one click away. The closest to "a graph next to the thing you are reading".
  • Obsidian, Graph view: a global graph of every note and a local graph of the open one, with filters and colour groups to keep a large graph legible.
  • Kumu: a tool for mapping systems and stakeholders, where selecting an element opens a side profile and the map stays put. That is the panel pattern here.

Risks

  • The 10-second read is the weakest of the three; the graph rewards people who already decided to look.
  • It overlaps /skills, which already lets a visitor pick a project and see its parts (P5-05). Two graphs that differ by one column will confuse.
  • Two panels on one screen: the project panel and the concierge drawer both want the right edge at desktop and the bottom on phones, and the pill sits over the panel's foot at 1280 unless the panel leaves room.
  • Keyboard and screen-reader cost: the List view has to carry the whole page, as it does on /skills.
B

A grid of case files

Each project is a file: a tab with who it was for, a large cover drawn from its own architecture diagram, one number in a band, then the title. The category chips the page has today stay.

What a hiring manager sees in 10 s

Three titles, three pictures of how each thing is wired, and three results at desktop before any scroll: 55 features passing in one day, a case study on the way, 2 days to about 6 hours of client onboarding. Who each project was for sits on the tab, so the mix of client work and personal systems shows at a glance. On a phone the first file and its number fit the first screen. Of the three options this is the fastest read.

Cost to build on the current data

Reuses: ProjectsGrid as it is (its FILTERS, sortByOrder, the empty state), ProjectCard's preview band, which already shows metrics[0] when there is no thumbnail, and the authored diagrams in src/components/projects/diagrams/ via each project's architectureDiagram key. Fields: company, title, categories, metrics, caseStudy.timeframe and caseStudy.role, order.

New: a cover rendering of each diagram (static, small, no captions inside; each diagram has to be checked at card width); the tab on ProjectCard; a "not measured yet" band for Voice Concierge, and a choice for Mirrored Google Calendars and Cherished, which have no metrics at all (today's card shows their first category instead); a cover for Cherished, the one project with no diagram; a period on the projects with no case study, or no date line on those cards.

Size: small. One build feature and one check feature; no new state, no new client component.

What others have done

  • Pentagram, Work: large project images, each with a one-line description and the client's sector, browsable by type of client; the image carries the card.
  • Vercel, Customers: case-study cards that lead with the customer and a one-line outcome, often a number.
  • Linear, Customers: a grid of customer stories, each a company and a one-line headline.
  • Stripe, Customers: case studies browsable by business type and by industry, the closest to the chip row here.

Risks

  • The covers carry the page. Diagrams drawn for the deep-dive may not survive shrinking to card width; if most need a second drawing, the cost grows.
  • It looks most like what the site has now. If the brief is "less bland", B fixes the evidence, not the character; that is P6-02's job.
  • Uneven bands: the band shows each project's first metric. Agent Harness and GoHighLevel lead with results, some lead with a phrase ("1 thread" per customer, "3 levels"), Voice Concierge has none until measured, and two projects have no metrics at all.
C

A dated log

The projects become entries in one ledger, newest first, written the way the harness writes its progress file: when, which project, what changed and why. A rail filters by project.

What a hiring manager sees in 10 s

That he ships often and writes down why: four dated changes from one week of October, in plain sentences. It has the most voice of the three and it tells the "agents wrote the code, Michael ran the system" story without saying so. What it hides is range. The 2025 client integrations, the strongest business numbers, sit far below the fold, and nothing on the first screen says what any project is for.

Cost to build on the current data

Reuses: caseStudy.iterations (when, label, what, why, short), caseStudy.timeframe, slug, title, metrics; IterationTimeline already renders the same items on each deep-dive; the role periods in experience.ts.

New: a sortable date on every iteration (when is free text today: "Sep 2026", "Oct 13–15", "Oct 16 eve"), a LedgerEntry, the project filter (rail at desktop, select on phones), month grouping, and one entry each for the projects with no iterations.

Size: medium. A data change across all 17 iterations, then one component and its filter.

What others have done

  • Linear, Changelog: dated entries, each a short headline and a paragraph; the tone is the product's own.
  • PostHog, Changelog: a long dated log where every entry names the team and the product area it belongs to, the job the project label does here.
  • Keep a Changelog: the convention a log like this would follow: newest first, dated, grouped by kind of change.
  • Simon Willison's Weblog: a personal site that is a dated log of what he builds and learns, read for the reasoning as much as the result.
  • Brittany Chiang, Archive: a developer portfolio that keeps every project in a dated table under the curated cards.

Risks

  • The data is lopsided: 17 dated iterations, all in 3 of the 10 projects (Agent Harness, the music library, HitList), and 14 of them from October 2026. The other 7 projects get one line each, so the log reads as a few weeks of October.
  • Two projects have no date at all: Mirrored Google Calendars (freelance) and Cherished (Duke). They need one from Michael, or they can't be placed.
  • Recency wins: whatever changed last week leads, whether or not it is the best work to show.

Recommendation: B, the case files

Build B for /projects. The first readers are hiring managers and recruiters (brand.md), and B is the only option that shows them results before they click or scroll. It is also the cheapest: it reshapes the grid the site already has, and its covers come from the diagrams that already exist for 9 of the 10 projects, so it keeps the rule against fake screenshots with one gap to fill (Cherished).

Why not A now: /skills already answers "which parts did this project use" through its project chips (P5-05). A second graph would repeat that and lose the fast read. If Michael wants the merged picture, it belongs on /skills, as the next step of that graph (the v3 spec, § 12, leaves it to a later run on purpose).

Why not C now: it has the most character, but dated iterations exist for 3 projects out of 10. The log already lives where it is strongest: in each case study's iteration timeline. Revisit C when more projects have dated iterations; it could then be a "Recently" strip above the grid rather than the whole page.

To decide first: whether the diagrams read at card size. One cheap check settles it: render Agent Harness, GoHighLevel ↔ JobTread and the music library's diagrams in a card-width box and look at the 390 px screenshot.

What option A would share with the P5 graph

  • The data: the same clusters, nodes and edges in skills.ts, including every project:<slug> edge. No new data file; A only reads edges P5 already wrote.
  • The layout and view math: layoutGraph (3 columns from 1024 px, 2 below), edgePaths, curvePath, projectChain, and clampView, zoomAt, panBy, fitCluster in lib/graph/view.ts, with their unit tests.
  • The interaction model: SkillGraph's one selection, data-active and data-dim, the cross-cluster chain drawn on selection, Esc and a second click to clear, the aria-live line, the pan and zoom controls and keys (D100), the 640 px chip row (D102), and reduced motion with no tweened view.
  • The text alternative: the List view as the accessible and print version; A adds a projects section to it.
  • What A does not share: the layout's shape. D99 kept d3-force out of P5 and names this option as the case for reconsidering it: if projects must be placed by their connections, the hand layout gives way to a force layout.
  • What A adds: projects promoted from chips to nodes (a seventh container in the layout) and a panel. Which is why, if it is ever built, it should replace the chips on /skills rather than become a second graph on /projects.
